How Does Starbucks Make Money From Gift Cards?

When you load $25 onto a coffee app, you hand over cash before you get a single drink. Millions of customers do that at once.

A balance that never stops

At September 28, 2025, Starbucks reported $1,751.7 million, about $1.75 billion, in stored value card and loyalty program deferred revenue. About $1.6 billion of it was classified as current.

Deferred revenue means the company has been paid but has not yet served the drink. Until you spend it, the money sits on the balance sheet.

Why that is useful

The card terms say the value is not insured by the FDIC and does not earn interest. US cards have no expiration date.

For the company, it is cash in hand before the sale. As a pure illustration, an assumed 4% return on $1,751.7 million would be about $70 million a year. That is an example, not a reported figure.

Money that never comes back

Some balances never get spent. Companies call that breakage. Starbucks booked breakage revenue of $215.0 million in fiscal 2023, $207.6 million in fiscal 2024 and $222.4 million in fiscal 2025.

Add the three years and you get $645.0 million. The latest year alone works out to about $609,000 a day.

Stars have a clock too

Loyalty stars generally expire after six to twelve months, depending on the market. That nudges members to come back and use them.

Put together, the card and app system does two jobs. It brings cash in early, and it keeps customers returning.

Think about your own phone. A balance sitting in an app makes the next coffee feel free, and a free-feeling coffee is easy to say yes to. The company does not need a trick for that. It only needs the balance to be there when you walk past a store.
