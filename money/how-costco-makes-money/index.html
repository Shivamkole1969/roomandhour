How Does Costco Make Money? It's the Membership

Walk through a Costco and the prices look like the whole story. They are not. The bigger story sits at the front door, where you scan a card.

The fee is the profit engine

In fiscal 2026, Costco reported $5.9 billion in membership fees. Its operating income for the same year was $11.7 billion. So the fees alone equal roughly half of what the company earned from operations.

That is odd for a retailer, because the fee is a tiny slice of what it collects. Net sales for the 52 weeks ended August 30, 2026 were $297 billion. The fees come to about 2% of that.

Thin margins on purpose

Operating income was about 3.9% of net sales, under four cents on each dollar. Picture a $200 cart. After all costs, the warehouse keeps under $8 of it.

Run it the other way and the fee looks huge. At that margin, earning $5.9 billion from selling goods alone would take around $150 billion of extra sales. That is a simplified illustration, not a reported number.

Why renewals matter more than sales

A fee only works if people pay it again. Costco's renewal rate was 92.3% in the US and Canada and 89.8% worldwide in fiscal 2026. Out of 100 US and Canadian members, about 92 come back.

The company ended the year with 84.1 million paid members, and the US annual fee is $65. Keep prices low, keep members happy, and the renewals keep landing.

What it means for the shelf

Because the fee covers so much of the profit, the shelves do not need fat markups. Low prices bring people in, low prices make them renew, and renewals pay the bills. Each part feeds the next one.

The book follows that loop further, including how it shapes what Costco chooses to sell and what it refuses to raise.
