Is McDonald's Really a Real Estate Business?

The burger is the product you see. The business behind it looks more like a landlord with a famous tenant list.

Almost everyone is a franchisee

McDonald's had 45,356 restaurants at the end of 2025, and approximately 95% of them were franchised. That works out to about 43,000 restaurants run by other owners.

Those owners pay the company. According to its annual report, franchisees pay mainly rent and royalties based on a percent of sales, with specified minimum rent payments, plus initial fees.

Where the revenue comes from

In 2025, total revenues were $26.9 billion. Revenues from franchised restaurants were $16.5 billion, about 62% of the total. Sales at company-operated restaurants were $9.7 billion.

The franchised side is also far more profitable. Franchised margins were $13.9 billion, which is about 84 cents of every franchised revenue dollar.

The land angle

McDonald's owned approximately 56% of the land and 80% of the buildings for restaurants in its consolidated markets on December 31, 2025. The company says it generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the site.

That is where the landlord idea comes from. A franchisee runs the kitchen. McDonald's holds the property and collects a share.

Rent is the biggest piece

One quarter shows the mix. In the first quarter of 2025, franchised revenues included $2.3 billion in rents, $1.3 billion in royalties and $18 million in initial fees. Rent made up about 63% of that total.

Spread across the quarter's 90 days, rent alone came to roughly $25 million a day, by our calculation.

Why does this matter? A burger chain's sales can rise and fall with prices and tastes. Rent is steadier, because it is tied to a lease and a share of sales rather than to a single menu item. It also explains why the company can look quiet while its franchise owners do the daily hard work.
