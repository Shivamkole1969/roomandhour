Why Is Costco's Hot Dog Still $1.50? It's a Lure

Two items in a Costco warehouse almost never change price. One is a hot dog and soda. The other is a roasted chicken. Both are doing a job.

A price frozen since 1985

The hot dog and soda combo has cost $1.50 since 1985, and it was still $1.50 in 2026. In 2026 the chain added a water option to the combo. The price stayed put.

More than 245 million combos were sold in fiscal 2025. Spread over a year, that is roughly 671,000 a day. At $1.50 each, the total comes to about $367 million. That figure is our own calculation, not a reported sales number.

The chicken does the same trick

Costco sold 157.4 million rotisserie chickens worldwide in fiscal 2025, up from 154 million the year before. The price was still $4.99 in 2026.

Multiply those two numbers and you get about $785 million in chicken sales. Again, that is a rough calculation, not a line from a report.

Giving up a little to gain a lot

In a 2015 analyst call, Costco's CFO said the company was willing to give up $30 million to $40 million a year in gross margin to keep the chicken at $4.99. That is a real cost, and the company accepts it.

Costco even built a processing plant in Nebraska to control supply. It cost about $450 million, a big bet to protect one cheap item.

The payoff is the membership

Management has described the low price as an investment that drives membership. A shopper who sees a hot dog for $1.50 trusts the rest of the warehouse a little more. That trust helps people renew their cards.

So the question is not whether the hot dog makes money. It is what the hot dog buys. The book looks at where else this logic shows up.
