Is Apple Really a Services Company? The Numbers

Apple sells devices you can hold. Yet a quieter line in its annual report may matter just as much.

The phone still leads

In fiscal 2025, Apple's total net sales were $416.2 billion. The iPhone brought in $209.6 billion, which is about half. Products overall were $307.0 billion.

Mac was $33.7 billion, iPad was $28.0 billion, and Wearables, Home and Accessories was $35.7 billion, down 4%.

Compared with the year before, Products grew about 4%, from $294.9 billion to $307.0 billion. The iPhone rose about 4% too. Steady, but not fast. Devices are a big base, and a big base grows slowly.

Services is smaller but growing

Services net sales were $109.2 billion, up 14% from $96.2 billion a year earlier. Growth came mainly from advertising, the App Store and cloud services.

That is about 26% of net sales, or about a third of the size of Products.

Think of what sits inside that line. Ads, app purchases and cloud storage all depend on people already owning the hardware. Each new phone in a pocket becomes a place to sell more, month after month, long after the device itself was paid for.

The margin gap

Gross margin tells a different story. Products earned 36.8%, around 37 cents on each dollar. Services earned 75.4%, around 75 cents.

Services produced $82.3 billion of Apple's total gross margin of $195.2 billion, about 42%. Its gross profit equals roughly 73% of what Products earned, from far less revenue.

The App Store cut

One piece of Services is the commission on apps. Under Apple's Small Business Program, announced in November 2020, developers earning up to $1 million a year pay 15%. Above that, the standard rate is 30%.

Devices bring people in, and then the services keep earning from them.

That also explains why Apple cares about keeping its users inside one system. A buyer who stays brings in service revenue each year, and the margin on that revenue is about double.
